OUR TECHNOLOGY

The process behind
the potential.

An approach that begins with a Material Recovery Facility (MRF) for sorting and preparation, followed by thermal conversion and product recovery for suitable feedstock.

THE CORE PRINCIPLE

Suitable material
+ controlled heat
+ product recovery

Explore the five stages of a typical pyrolysis process. The exact plant configuration depends on feedstock and project requirements.

  1. Prepare: Incoming materials are assessed, sorted, and prepared in a Material Recovery Facility (MRF). Unsuitable or problematic materials are removed before suitable feedstock enters the pyrolysis process. Preparation helps match the material to the conversion process.
  2. Feed & heat: Prepared material enters the enclosed reactor through controlled feeding equipment. Heat is applied while limiting oxygen in the conversion zone. Feed rate, sealing, and heating requirements are part of the equipment design.
  3. Convert: Heat breaks down suitable material into vapours, non-condensable gases, and solid residue. Process conditions influence the outputs and their characteristics. The reaction is different from burning the feedstock; plant heating still needs its own controls.
  4. Condense: Vapours pass through cooling and condensation stages. Condensable fractions are recovered as liquid pyrolysis oil for storage and quality assessment. Fuel quality and suitability must be assessed against the intended industrial application.
  5. Recover: Non-condensable gas is handled for potential process heating. Char is cooled and collected. Treatment, monitoring, and product testing remain essential. Recovery pathways depend on product quality, local demand, and applicable requirements.

A CLOSER LOOK

Understanding
the details.

Can all waste be processed?

Feedstock suitability must be assessed. The overview focuses on suitable plastic streams and highlights the importance of sorting and removing problematic materials. Mixed industrial waste requires its own assessment.

What does the process produce?

The primary output groups described in the overview are pyrolysis oil, combustible gas, and char. Yield and quality vary with feedstock and operating conditions.

Does pyrolysis mean zero emissions?

No. The conversion reaction takes place with limited oxygen, but heating, gas handling, and other plant operations require emissions treatment, monitoring, and compliance with applicable conditions.

Is the process self-powered?

Recovered gas may be reused for process heating. Startup energy and other operating inputs remain part of project design; net energy requirements need a project-specific assessment.

Are renewable diesel, SAF, or hydrogen current products?

This website focuses on the pyrolysis outputs described in the company overview. Further conversion into other fuels requires additional technology, product qualification, and confirmation of project scope.
